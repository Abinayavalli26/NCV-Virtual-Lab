A. Aim

To determine the Motor Nerve Conduction Velocity (MNCV) of the median nerve in a human subject using surface electrode stimulation and recording, and to record the latencies, amplitude and conduction velocity of the evoked Compound Muscle Action Potential (CMAP).

B. Apparatus Required

Nerve studied: Median Nerve (Motor) Muscle recorded: Abductor Pollicis Brevis (APB)

C. Introduction to NCV

Nerve Conduction Velocity (NCV) measures the speed at which an electrical impulse travels along a peripheral nerve. It is a standard electrodiagnostic tool for assessing the functional integrity of motor and sensory nerve fibres, and is widely used in diagnosing carpal tunnel syndrome, peripheral neuropathy and nerve entrapment syndromes.

  1. The nerve is electrically stimulated at the skin surface.
  2. An action potential is generated and travels along the nerve.
  3. The muscle response is recorded with surface electrodes.
  4. The recorded response is the Compound Muscle Action Potential (CMAP).
  5. The time until the response appears is the latency.
  6. Conduction velocity uses the distance between stimulation sites and the difference in their latencies.

Animated impulse along the median nerve (schematic)

D. Principle of Motor Nerve Conduction Study

A motor nerve is stimulated at two or more points along its course, and the resulting CMAP is recorded from the muscle it supplies. The time delay between the stimulus artifact and the CMAP onset is the latency.

Distal Latency (Lat 1): recorded from the wrist stimulation site (S1). Proximal Latency (Lat 2): recorded from the elbow site (S2), including the extra conduction time along the segment. The difference between them is used to calculate NCV.

E. Formula for NCV

CV (m/s) = Distance between stimulation sites (mm) / [Proximal Latency (ms) − Distal Latency (ms)]

Distance: surface distance between wrist and elbow stimulation sites (mm). Proximal latency: at elbow (ms). Distal latency: at wrist (ms).

Illustrative example (not an experimental observation): Distance = 200 mm; Distal latency = 3 ms; Proximal latency = 7 ms → CV = 200 / (7 − 3) = 50 m/s.

F. Standard Stimulation Sites – Median Nerve

S1 – Wrist: between the Palmaris Longus and Flexor Carpi Radialis tendons, about 1 cm proximal to the distal wrist crease.
S2 – Elbow: at the elbow crease, medial to the biceps tendon and brachial artery.
S3 – Axilla (optional): at the axilla, used for an additional segment.

G. Electrode Placement

Active (Ra): over the belly of the APB. Reference (Rr): over the APB tendon, distal to Ra. Ground (GND): over the dorsum of the hand. These are recording electrodes, not the stimulation probe. See them in the Experiment section.

H. CMAP Waveform and Latency

Latency is measured from the stimulus artifact to the CMAP onset. In the illustrated example, proximal latency is greater than distal latency, as expected for a longer conduction path. All waveform values shown are illustrative. Open the Experiment section to explore the interactive waveform.
